03 / COMMON QUESTIONS

Geek Protocol FAQ

What is Geek Protocol?

Geek Protocol helps people learn about Kaspa and other subjects through short lessons, questions, explanations and games. Geek HQ is the active web product. Its learning-first approach began with a father making index cards to help his son learn to read.

Read the mission and approach →

What does Proof of Learning mean?

Proof of Learning is Geek's application model for connecting a learning attempt to its content, rules and accepted result. It records participation and performance under those rules. It does not certify lasting mastery, provide accreditation or replace Kaspa's proof-of-work consensus.

Read the Proof of Learning model →

Do I need a wallet or GEEK to start?

No. Begin the free learning path and quizzes without a wallet or token purchase. A saved Geek account uses a passkey for access. Optional wallet features have separate requirements; entering a receiving address does not prove ownership of that wallet.

Start with five simple steps →

What is the GEEK token?

GEEK is a KRC-20 token on Kaspa Mainnet. The pinned specification is a maximum of 144,000,000,000 GEEK, eight decimals, zero premint and a 100,000 GEEK per mint limit. These are deployment parameters, not a live circulating-supply report. Check the deployment identity and current availability before any wallet-approved mint.

Read the token specification →

Can I withdraw Alpha credits or earn Mainnet GEEK by playing?

Alpha credits and career XP are internal application records. They cannot be withdrawn or automatically exchanged for GEEK. Public Mainnet treasury rewards are not live. Future rewards require separate funding, eligibility rules, security review and approval.

Read the economy and proposed rewards →

Is Geek Protocol part of the Kaspa core team?

Geek Protocol is an independent Kaspa community project. It teaches Kaspa concepts and builds related tools. Its community links and acknowledgments do not imply endorsement, sponsorship or a formal partnership with Kaspa researchers or other projects.

Meet the contributors and acknowledged builders →

Has Geek Protocol been independently audited?

The application penetration test and Mainnet treasury and signing audit remain outstanding. Internal automated checks are implementation evidence; they are not an independent security audit, certification or external grade.

Who builds Geek, and how can I contribute?

The Hall of Thanks recognizes community support and the builders and tools acknowledged by Geek. You can share feedback, report an issue or submit an original sourced question. How do Geek Mini, Geek Jr and Geek Wallet fit in?

These are separate companion projects: Geek Mini offers quick quizzes, Geek Jr focuses on early reading, and Geek Wallet tracks public Kaspa addresses. The Kaspa Live Widget is another community tool. Each has its own implementation and release status.
