# Community and Contributor Charter

**Document:** GEEK-COMM-001 · **Version:** 0.1 · **Status:** Founder review draft — not adopted

**Source baseline:** HQ commit `b7521c6db2087d0c40580afa443104f33f4cd212` (November 16, 2026 snapshot). This draft distinguishes recorded implementation from proposed requirements. It does not revise the published whitepaper or activate a release gate.

## Purpose and status

Geek Protocol grows through useful learning material, code, testing, artwork and community help. This proposed charter explains how contributions should be evaluated and credited. It is a founder review draft, not an adopted moderation policy, employment agreement, payment schedule or transfer of intellectual property.

The current CCE bank-and-economy hold stays in place. A visible contribution form or draft charter does not reopen intake, publication or contributor credits. Resumption requires 1,000 distinct source-reviewed concepts in each of eight categories and separate economy approval.

## Proposed community expectations

Participants should discuss ideas and corrections respectfully. Disagreement about an answer or release is welcome when it identifies evidence. Harassment, targeted threats, impersonation, doxxing, spam, phishing and deliberate publication of protected learner or assessment material should be grounds for review.

Never request another person's seed phrase, private key, recovery code or account proof. An official-looking avatar or direct message does not establish authority to receive money or secrets. Use the project's published channels to verify a request.

Accessibility reports and beginner questions are contributions. A person should be able to participate in learning and discussion without buying tokens.

## Proposed contribution pathway

A contribution begins with a scoped proposal or existing task. The author should identify what they created, what they reused, relevant sources and licenses, and any tools or generated material that materially affect review. Tool output needs the same factual and provenance checks as human-authored work.

For a question, include its objective, answer, plausible alternatives, original explanation, specific source support and context date or version. A reviewer should check that the cited source supports the exact answer. Similar wording is not the only duplicate risk; repeated objectives need review.

Authorship, fact checking and final acceptance are different roles. An author should not be the sole approver of their own item. Founder contributions receive the same separate fact check. Automated checks identify problems but do not certify truth or originality.

## Credit, revisions and rights

Attribution should follow a documented contribution record. Significant revisions should retain provenance and explain material changes. Authors should be able to report misattribution or factual errors through the published project channels without posting private account material.

Submitting a draft does not imply a new blanket intellectual-property assignment. Use the repository's applicable license and contribution terms, and resolve unclear permissions before publishing third-party text or art. This charter creates no new license to project characters, trademarks or contributor work.

Retired questions should remain traceable for prior grading and corrections. A replacement should not silently change the meaning of an earlier accepted answer.

## Rewards and conflicts

Contributor rates are unselected. Token holdings, past Alpha credits, volunteer hours and public use of a question do not automatically create a payable claim. A future program needs a funded rate, an eligible-use definition, attribution after revision, caps, fraud controls and dispute terms before people rely on it.

Reviewers should disclose relationships or financial interests that could affect a decision and seek a separate reviewer when necessary. Tokens do not currently confer HQ governance or treasury powers.

## Proposed moderation and appeal process

An assigned maintainer should assess the behavior and evidence, preserve necessary context with restricted access, and choose a proportionate response. Possible responses include clarification, correction, removal of material, feature restriction or account action under an approved policy.

Before adoption, the project must name who can act, how an affected participant receives a reason, where an appeal goes, and what information can be retained or disclosed. No response deadline or universal appeal right is promised by this draft. Safety incidents and technical security reports need their appropriate private route rather than public reproduction.
