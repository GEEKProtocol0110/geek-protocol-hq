# Roadmap and Release Acceptance

**Document:** GEEK-ROAD-001 · **Version:** 0.1 · **Status:** Founder review draft — not adopted

**Source baseline:** HQ commit `b7521c6db2087d0c40580afa443104f33f4cd212` (November 16, 2026 snapshot). This draft distinguishes recorded implementation from proposed requirements. It does not revise the published whitepaper or activate a release gate.

## Purpose

This roadmap organizes work by evidence needed to release it. It supplies no promised launch dates. A source implementation, passing internal checks, merged commit, verified deployment, physical-device acceptance and independent review are distinct milestones.

The starting point is Public Alpha. HQ offers learning, timed quizzes, progress, social formats, local A.C.E. practice and GEEK wallet-approved minting. Geek Jr and Geek Mini are related products with separate repositories and release evidence. Features must not be marked complete across all three because HQ changed.

## Stage 1: dependable learning and play

The immediate goal is a clear first visit and a reliable return: readable lessons, useful explanations, visible account status, compact active games, understandable errors and recoverable progress.

Acceptance should include actual phone and tablet use, keyboard access, reduced-motion behavior, account switching, connection interruptions and resuming a local A.C.E. turn. Browser automation is useful evidence but does not establish every credential provider or physical device.

A.C.E.'s seven local opponents and browser-local Arena continuity are implemented in the recent game releases. Their records remain personal practice, without cross-device sync or ranked authority. Adding the original character artwork to a product surface is separate work from locating its archived image.

## Stage 2: trustworthy content

Audit all eight categories for factual support, distinct objectives, duplicates, ambiguity, changing context and licensing. Preserve issued answer keys and concept aliases when retiring or replacing questions.

The CCE hold requires 1,000 distinct source-reviewed concepts per category and separately approved economy rules. A ranked season capacity plan is a different measure and cannot waive that hold. Imported row counts and automated checks must not be presented as independently reviewed capacity.

Completion evidence should identify the accepted artifact, editorial method, reviewer disposition, remaining disputes and exact counts under that method.

## Stage 3: private ranked acceptance

The held private-bank and season work needs restricted artifact delivery, immutable content versions, eligibility and mismatch rules, historical grading, delayed reveals, rotation and leak handling. Publicly exposed questions are not made secret by moving them later.

Acceptance needs end-to-end tests of actual artifact loading and live season rules, failure and rollback behavior, and review of the assembled release. The existing private-proof payout guard must be preserved. Synthetic private context in a test fixture is not a production cutover.

## Stage 4: operational readiness

Assign privileged roles, verify monitoring, isolate preview data and credentials, and rehearse recovery. Recovery must cover source, application data, provider configuration and unresolved journals within a defined scope.

Security acceptance requires the exact candidate and relevant findings, remediation and retest. Existing internal tests do not replace an independent application or signer assessment. Remaining owners and retention requirements must be recorded rather than inferred.

## Stage 5: bounded economics

A later activation requires accepted private competition, verified funding, approved eligibility and schedule, bounded exposure, isolated signer controls, reconciliation, incident procedures, independent review and explicit founder authorization.

Current payout code, temporary caps and passing fixture tests are preparation. Public settlement, CCE and collectible ownership remain disabled. A documentation merge cannot enable them.

## Stage 6: additional products and institutions

Creator compensation, stores, collectibles, governance, school pilots and professional programs each need a scoped proposal and acceptance criteria. School use requires agreed roles, consent, data handling, content suitability and a measured objective before recruitment. No institutional endorsement or agreement is implied.

## Proposed release record

Each release should retain scope, exact commit, tests and limits, approved gates, preview evidence, merge result, deployment identity, public verification, unresolved device acceptance and rollback or pause instructions. Stop a gate when its evidence is missing; keep unrelated free learning usable where safe.

No single engagement metric proves learning. Track content quality and disputed answers, recovery success and, in a consented study, delayed retention alongside ordinary product usage.
